      METHODOLOGICAL FOUNDATION

      Three-Stage Inference Architecture

      STAGE 01

      Passive Lexical Extraction

      The URL string is canonicalized without outbound network requests. 15 numerical and structural features are extracted (host length, dot count, hyphens, entropy, @ userinfo, sensitive keywords).

      STAGE 02

      Semi-Supervised Graph ML

      Features are standardized and projected onto an RBF affinity graph. LabelSpreading propagates known label evidence along data density manifolds, capturing pattern clusters without deep training costs.

      STAGE 03

      Sugeno Fuzzy Synthesis

      Continuous graph proximity and lexical risk are mapped onto Low, Medium, and High fuzzy membership sets. Nine Sugeno rules compute a weighted-average composite risk index with clear auditability.

      Security Misconceptions: What URL Analysis Does NOT Prove

      1. "HTTPS means the website is legitimate."

      HTTPS indicates encrypted transport (TLS), but free automated certificates (Let's Encrypt) mean over 80% of modern phishing domains use valid HTTPS. HTTPS is a lexical scheme feature, never proof of safety.

      2. "Low risk score guarantees the website is safe."

      A low risk score simply indicates the absence of suspicious lexical heuristics and graph distance from known threat templates. Sophisticated spear-phishing or compromised reputable domains can evade lexical analysis entirely.

      3. "Graph ML is identical to Deep Graph Neural Networks (GNN)."

      PhishGraph uses classical LabelSpreading with an RBF affinity matrix and normalized graph Laplacian. It does NOT use deep graph convolutional networks (GCN/GAT) or backpropagation.
